A guide to reading the evidence beside an answer

Open the source before you trust the claim.

A citation is a route to a page, not a verdict on the sentence next to it. The useful question is narrower: does this page support this particular claim, under these conditions?

By Alex Rostovtsev · SEO & AI visibility

Keep these together

Claim → passage → judgement → next check.

If one of those disappears, the citation gets much easier to overread.

01

The method

Read for support, not for resemblance

Start with the exact answer claim. Copy it without improving the wording. Then open the cited page and identify the publisher, page date if there is one, and the passage that appears relevant.

Do not stop when the page mentions the same topic. A page can be about opening hours without supporting “open 24 hours”; about pricing without supporting “no setup fee”; or about a research result without supporting the broader conclusion someone drew from it.

Read the qualification around the passage. Words such as weekday, selected plans, in this sample, up to and subject to availability often carry more evidential weight than the headline.

Use one of five outcomes

  • Supports
  • Supports part of it
  • Contradicts
  • Doesn’t establish it
  • Not checked
  1. 1

    Copy the precise claim

    Keep numbers, locations, time limits and conditions. “Offers weekend access” and “is open every weekend” are not the same claim.

  2. 2

    Identify the source

    Who published the page? Is it current? Is it the business speaking about itself, a directory repeating a listing, or a paper describing a study?

  3. 3

    Find the relevant passage

    Record the text that might support the claim. If you cannot find a passage, do not promote the citation from “attached” to “supporting”.

  4. 4

    Read the limits around it

    Check nearby sentences, footnotes, dates and scope. A qualification is part of the evidence, not clutter around it.

  5. 5

    Write the judgement and the next check

    If support is partial or contradictory, state exactly what remains open. The next check may be another page, a newer policy, or direct confirmation from the business.

02

Worked examples · fictional

Morrow Coworking

All names, passages and URLs below are made up. They are examples of citation logic, not reports about a real business.

Claim

“Morrow Coworking members can enter the building on weekdays from 07:00 to 21:00.”

Source excerpt

“Member access: Monday–Friday, 07:00–21:00. Reception is staffed from 09:00.”

Fictional source: https://morrow-coworking.example/access

Supports

The days and hours match. The reception detail is separate and does not weaken the access claim.

Next check: none for this narrow claim, beyond confirming that the page is still current.

Claim

“Members have round-the-clock access.”

Source excerpt

“Member access: Monday–Friday, 07:00–21:00. Weekend entry may be arranged for private events.”

Fictional source: https://morrow-coworking.example/access

Doesn’t establish it

The page describes limited weekday access and conditional weekend entry. Nothing here establishes 24/7 access.

Next check: look for a separate membership tier or building-access policy before deciding whether the claim is simply unsupported or false.

Claim

“All meeting-room bookings can be cancelled without charge.”

Source excerpt

“Cancellations made at least 48 hours before the booking are free. Later cancellations are charged at 50% of the room fee.”

Fictional source: https://morrow-coworking.example/terms

Contradicts

The source explicitly gives a late-cancellation charge, so the absolute claim is wrong.

Next check: correct the claim to include the 48-hour condition and confirm that the policy date is current.

03

What kind of evidence is this?

A link can play several different roles

Attached citation

A source link shown next to an answer. It tells you where to look. It does not by itself prove that the page supports the exact wording.

Unlinked reference

A named source, report or business with no direct route to the underlying page. Treat it as a lead until you find the source.

The source’s own claim

An official page can establish what the business says about its hours, service or policy. It does not automatically provide independent verification of a performance claim.

Independently verified fact

A claim checked against evidence that is appropriate to the question. The right source depends on what is being established.

Official business page

Best for the business’s current service scope, published pricing, locations, policies and terms—provided the page is current.

Third-party directory

Useful for discovering a listing or comparing what outsiders show. It may lag behind the business, omit conditions or copy stale information.

Research paper

Useful for a study’s methods and reported findings. A paper does not automatically establish that its result generalises to your market, page or live search system.
